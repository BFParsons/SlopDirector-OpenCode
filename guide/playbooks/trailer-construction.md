# Trailer construction

## Scope and precedence

Use this playbook for trailers, teasers and trailer-style spots, including when
the brief names a film director rather than a trailer agency. Within trailer
work, these creative constraints take precedence over conflicting timing,
music, card-count or categorical "never" recipes in a style profile. User
direction and technical validation still apply. A named agency is a reference
library; identify the exact campaign execution being studied.

## Flexible constraints

- Act lengths, average shot length and minimum style ranges are review guides.
  Plan around the requested duration and selected material.
- There is no required on-grid percentage, hit per cut, accent per dialogue
  ending, tempo doubling, or fixed music entrance. Use phrase boundaries,
  anticipation, holds and deliberate off-beat edits.
- There is no fixed card count or blanket ban on contextual labels. Use text
  only when its function and reading time justify it.
- At the current 30 fps verification rate, shots below six frames remain
  technical errors. Six-to-nine-frame accents are readability warnings, not
  automatic plan failures; reserve them for deliberate felt rather than read
  moments. Important information and dialogue need enough time to register.
- The 120 visual-segment and 32 audio-overlay ceilings are capacity budgets,
  not targets. Keep the eight AI-shot cost guard and agreed generation scope.

## Plan and exploration

Record the selling idea and a named reference execution in plan notes. Rank
actual moments by dramatic function, expression/action, novelty, source quality,
usable sound and handles. Mark unverified desired footage as a sourcing need;
do not present a speculative quote as found dialogue.

For a new composition, an approved plan can include a bounded exploration phase:
source selects, audition exact music passages, and build a 15–20-second proof.
Carry out work already authorized by the user; request approval only for a
material expansion beyond that scope. Revise detailed timing after the material
is known rather than forcing weak matches into speculative slots.

Compare two or three exact recordings or arrangements for attitude, dialogue
space, development and ending. Record version, passage, source and listening
status. Tempo analysis supports an audition; it cannot replace it. Inspect and
listen after meaningful time stretching or music edits.

## Edit and review

Keep dialogue, music, effects and ambience separately adjustable through finishing.
Choose the foreground sound at each moment. Build a phrase/event map alongside
picture timing, including preparation, interruption, release and useful tails.

Review the proof for a memorable moment, readable setup/payoff, new information,
musical identity and coherent type/motion. Expand it only after that mechanism
works. A proof is a production experiment, not a substitute for the final review.

Report technical verification, inspected picture/motion, and listening review
separately. When listening is unavailable, mark musical fit and mix quality as
unverified; do not describe an audio-analysis pass as a listening pass or a
finished creative review. Record intentional exceptions to warnings with reasons.
